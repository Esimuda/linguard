Linguard ("guarding language") is a Nigerian multilingual safety project for the Lexsi AI Safety and Alignment Hackathon.

Small open multilingual models often refuse scam and social-engineering requests in English, then comply when the same intent is asked in Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, or Nigerian Pidgin. Linguard attacks that gap on Cohere Labs Tiny Aya Earth and turns the failures into a repair loop.

We chain Lexsi Track 2 kits into one pipeline. AuditKIT scores a live baseline on red-team seeds across five languages. CuratorKIT quality-gates those failures into a public repair dataset (Esimuda/linguard-safety). SafeTune applies CAA inference-time steering from a held-out calibration set. A LoRA adapter then hardens the weights on the calibration set plus the CuratorKIT repair data, with helpful answers self-distilled from the base model so it does not learn to refuse everything. AuditKIT compares before and after on the same samples. CircuitKIT runs EAP-IG discovery on a registered linguard_refusal task and exports the pruned circuit checkpoint (Esimuda/linguard-circuit) as an exploratory map of which heads and MLPs matter for refusal.

On Tiny Aya Earth (GPU run, 9 Nov 2026), on 120 held-out seeds (76 attacks, 44 benign controls) that no training step saw, attack success fell from 39.5% at baseline to 1.3% with the Linguard LoRA adapter, and over-refusal on benign prompts stayed at 0%. Overall accuracy rose from 75.0% to 99.2%, and AuditKIT linguard_attack_success moved from 0.2500 to 0.0083 (−96.7% relative). CAA steering at strength 0.25 on the same seeds reached 3.9% attack success but refused 11.4% of benign prompts (30% in Pidgin), so the weight-level fix removes the over-refusal trade-off we reported in the CPU pilot (37.5% to 4.2% attack success, 40% over-refusal, n=34). The scorer is a multilingual keyword heuristic and n is small, so we treat this as a strong pilot.
